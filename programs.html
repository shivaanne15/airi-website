Programs & care

Intestinal Failure and Rehabilitation Programs

AIRI provides physician-led, multidisciplinary care for patients with intestinal failure, short bowel syndrome, and complex nutrition needs. Our programs support patients across the continuum of care, from intestinal rehabilitation and complication prevention to evaluation and coordination of advanced surgical and transplant care when needed.

Explore our programs

Intestinal Failure Program

Coordinated medical and nutrition care for patients with intestinal failure, including clinical monitoring, central line care, and support with the transition from hospital to home.

Explore the Intestinal Failure Program →

Short Bowel Syndrome

Specialized care for patients with short bowel syndrome, focused on improving intestinal function and absorption, managing nutrition and hydration needs, and reducing dependence on medical nutrition and fluid support when clinically appropriate.

Learn about short bowel syndrome care →

Intestinal Rehabilitation

A multidisciplinary approach to improving intestinal function, optimizing nutrition and hydration, managing complications, and helping patients achieve greater nutritional independence.

Explore Intestinal Rehabilitation →

Intestinal Transplant

For patients with severe or life-threatening complications of intestinal failure, AIRI helps identify when intestinal transplant evaluation may be appropriate and coordinates referral to specialized transplant centers.

Learn about intestinal transplant →

How these programs fit together

Intestinal failure is the broader condition in which the intestine cannot absorb enough nutrients, fluids, or electrolytes without medical support. Short bowel syndrome is one common cause. Intestinal rehabilitation is the treatment approach that helps the remaining intestine work as well as it can, and intestinal transplant is an advanced option when other treatment can no longer keep a patient safe and healthy.

Not every patient needs every program. Our team evaluates each patient's needs and coordinates the appropriate care with their existing gastroenterologist, surgeon, and hospital team.

Refer a patient or ask a question

Hospitals, LTACHs, gastroenterologists, and surgeons across northern California are encouraged to refer early, before the next admission. Patients and families are welcome to contact us with questions.
