About AIRI

The Advanced Intestinal Rehabilitation Institute (AIRI) is a physician-led program providing coordinated care for patients with intestinal failure, short bowel syndrome, and complex nutrition needs.

AIRI was created to bridge the gap between hospital care and life at home, bringing together medical care, nutrition support, monitoring, education, and care coordination closer to the communities patients call home.

Why AIRI exists

Founded by Rajesh Kunadharaju, MD, FACP, FCCP

Dr. Kunadharaju founded AIRI after years of caring for medically complex patients across hospital, critical-care, rehabilitation, and post-acute settings.

Through this work, he saw how patients with intestinal failure, short bowel syndrome, complex nutrition needs, and dependence on parenteral nutrition can face fragmented care — particularly during the transition from hospital to home.

He created AIRI to provide a more coordinated, patient-centered pathway that brings together medical care, nutrition support, monitoring, education, and the clinicians and caregivers involved in each patient's care.

Dr. Kunadharaju is AIRI's Founder and Medical Director and is board certified in Internal Medicine, Pulmonary Medicine, and Critical Care Medicine.

Our vision

AIRI's vision is to be a trusted, community-based resource for intestinal rehabilitation and specialized parenteral nutrition support, helping patients receive coordinated care closer to home.

Our goal is not simply to manage parenteral nutrition. We work to help each patient achieve the best possible level of nutritional stability, hydration, functional recovery, independence, and quality of life.

Our approach to care

Individualized care

Every patient's intestinal anatomy, diagnosis, nutrition needs, treatment goals, home environment, and support system are different. Care plans should reflect those individual needs.

Coordinated transitions

Going home on parenteral nutrition should not mean going home without support. AIRI coordinates with inpatient teams, home-infusion providers, home-health clinicians, patients, caregivers, and outpatient specialists.

Education and empowerment

Patients and caregivers need practical, understandable information about nutrition support, central-line safety, hydration, monitoring, warning signs, and when to contact their care team.

Collaboration

AIRI works alongside — not in place of — each patient's gastroenterologist, surgeon, primary care clinician, hospital team, dietitian, pharmacist, infusion provider, and other specialists.

Connecting hospital care with life at home

Complex intestinal and nutrition care often extends across multiple settings. AIRI helps connect the people involved in that care so patients and families have greater continuity as they transition from the hospital to ongoing care at home.

We coordinate with referring clinicians, hospitals, gastroenterologists, surgeons, dietitians, home-infusion pharmacies, home-health teams, and other specialists while supporting ongoing nutrition management, monitoring, education, and intestinal rehabilitation.
