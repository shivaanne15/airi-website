Intestinal Failure Program

AIRI provides coordinated medical and nutrition care for patients with intestinal failure and short bowel syndrome. Our physician-led program combines intestinal rehabilitation, total parenteral nutrition (TPN) and home parenteral nutrition (HPN) management, clinical monitoring, and hospital-to-home support to help patients improve intestinal function, prevent complications, and reduce dependence on parenteral nutrition when clinically appropriate.

Program goals

  • Improve intestinal function and the absorption of nutrients and fluids
  • Reduce dependence on parenteral nutrition and IV fluids when clinically appropriate
  • Prevent and manage complications related to intestinal failure, parenteral nutrition, and central venous access
  • Support long-term health and nutritional independence, including safe transition to home care and evaluation for surgical or transplant options when appropriate

What the program includes

Comprehensive evaluation

We assess intestinal anatomy and surgical history, nutrition and hydration status, oral and enteral intake, parenteral nutrition needs, medications, laboratory trends, and venous access to develop an individualized care plan.

Nutrition and parenteral nutrition management

We individualize oral, enteral, and parenteral nutrition based on intestinal anatomy, absorption, fluid and electrolyte needs, organ function, and laboratory trends, adjusting support as the patient's needs change.

Medication and intestinal rehabilitation

Medications and rehabilitation strategies are tailored to intestinal anatomy and symptoms to manage output, improve absorption, and support intestinal adaptation. Patients may also be evaluated for therapies such as GLP-2 analogues when appropriate.

Central-line care and ongoing monitoring

We focus on preserving central venous access and preventing complications while monitoring hydration, kidney and liver function, electrolytes, micronutrients, bone health, and tolerance of nutrition therapy.

Hospital-to-home transition

No patient with a diagnosis of intestinal failure is discharged on home parenteral nutrition until metabolic stability, patient or caregiver competency, venous access readiness, home logistics, and follow-up plans are fully documented.

Discharge readiness includes a finalized nutrition care plan, a stable electrolyte profile, functioning venous access, home-infusion coordination, written emergency instructions, and demonstrated ability to perform setup, aseptic technique, pump management, troubleshooting, and emergency response.

Escalation pathways

PathwayWhen it is used
Multidisciplinary conferenceNew referrals, unstable patients, catheter infections, PN adjustments, surgical candidates, and transplant consideration. Action items are documented and assigned.
Surgical reviewEarly review for patients whose anatomy or complications may benefit from reconstruction, bowel lengthening, fistula repair, ostomy revision, or correction of obstruction.
Transplant referralEarly referral for progressive PN-related liver dysfunction, recurrent life-threatening catheter sepsis, depletion of central venous access, or inability to maintain health despite optimized care.
Specialty co-managementHepatology, nephrology, infectious diseases, wound and ostomy services, behavioral health, and interventional radiology, as needed.

Service boundary. AIRI does not prescribe or longitudinally manage controlled substances. We assess symptoms, identify safety concerns, reconcile medications, communicate with the established prescriber, and coordinate referral to pain management, addiction medicine, or psychiatry when clinically indicated.
